The Overview

Designing for Hong Kong's smallest floor plans

Hong Kong is home to some of the world's most compact residential properties, where every square foot matters. Designing a small apartment isn't simply about choosing attractive furniture. It's about creating a functional, comfortable and visually spacious home through smart space planning, custom storage and multifunctional design.

Whether you're renovating a studio, a one-bedroom flat or a compact family home, hiring an experienced interior designer can dramatically improve daily living. The best studios understand how to maximise natural light, optimise layouts, integrate built-in cabinetry and create seamless living environments without compromising aesthetics.

This guide is brought to you by Small Space Glow, and forms part of our ongoing series on Hong Kong home renovation. You can also browse our companion roundup, 10 Best Interior Designers in Hong Kong, for a wider set of studios to compare.

The Case For Design

Why small apartments need great design more, not less

In a city where the median flat is under 500 square feet, every centimetre is a design decision. Good planning is the difference between a home that feels cramped and one that feels calm.

Perceived space compounds

Sightlines, light bounce and furniture scale do more for how big a room feels than its actual square footage ever will.

Storage has to be designed, not added

Retrofitted shelving looks like an afterthought. Storage planned into the layout from day one disappears into the walls.

One room, several jobs

A living room that's also a home office and a guest room needs furniture and zoning that can change roles in minutes.

Mistakes cost more per square foot

In a small flat, one badly placed wall or oversized sofa can eat ten percent of your usable floor area.

Building rules are stricter

Hong Kong's older buildings bring plumbing stacks, fire codes and management approvals that need specialist experience.

It affects daily wellbeing

Cluttered, poorly lit small spaces are linked to higher day-to-day stress, and thoughtful design is a quality-of-life upgrade.

Practical Advice

Interior design tips for small apartments

Simple, high-impact moves you can apply with or without hiring a designer.

01

Let the wall colour run into the ceiling

Carrying one colour from wall to ceiling removes the visual "lid" that makes low ceilings feel lower.

02

Choose furniture with visible legs

Sofas and beds raised off the floor let light pass underneath, which reads as more open floor space.

03

Mirror opposite a window, not beside it

Placed correctly, a mirror doubles the perceived light source instead of just reflecting a wall.

04

Use vertical storage before floor storage

Shelving that climbs to the ceiling keeps the floor plan clear and draws the eye upward.

05

Pick one accent colour, not three

A single recurring accent feels intentional; several competing accents read as visual clutter.

06

Zone with rugs, not walls

A rug can define a "living area" inside an open-plan room without losing a single square foot to partitions.
